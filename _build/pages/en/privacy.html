Privacy

Effective 20 November 2026. This English version is the binding one; translations are provided for convenience.

This page is the full version of what the app shows you under More → Privacy. The short version is on that screen; nothing here contradicts it.

Ridni is a tracker for your family. A parent belongs to one family; someone who helps — a grandmother, a nanny — can be in several, with their own role in each. The reason this policy is short is that the app does little: it stores what you record, it syncs it to the other adults in your family, and it does not sell, share, profile or advertise against any of it.

Who is responsible for your data. Andrii Sotnikov, Kyiv, Ukraine, who develops and runs Ridni. Write to privacy@ridni.app about anything on this page.

What you record, and who can see it

Sleep, feeds, drinks, diapers, pumping, baths, walks, teething and activities; weights, heights and head size; notes, temperatures, medications, vaccinations and doctor's appointments; pregnancy details such as the due date; the mother's own weight and height; and cycle entries — everything you enter is stored so that the adults in your family see it, and nobody else does. There is no public profile, no sharing link, and no feed.

Your family is the people you invited with a family code, each with a role: an owner, parents, and caregivers. A caregiver sees the children and can add records; they cannot see your cycle, the pregnancy, the mother's weight or her appointments, and they cannot change who is in the family. The mother's weight is hers: the other adults see it only after she turns sharing on. Who added which record is kept and shown, because in a shared log it matters who wrote it down.

What we store about your account

Your name, your email address, your profile photo if you add one, and a password that is stored only as a hash — we cannot read it. If you signed in with Google or Apple, we store the account identifier they give us and your email, and no password at all; for Apple we also keep the token Apple issues, so that we can disconnect Sign in with Apple when you delete the account. We also store your region, time zone, units, language and theme, because the app needs them to show dates, weights and temperatures the way you expect, and what you use cycle tracking for, if you answer that question. For each phone you use we keep its platform, the app version and when it last synced, so that records and reminders reach the right device.

What you record about your children

Name and nickname, date and time of birth, gender, birth details if you enter them (weeks of pregnancy, birth weight, length and head size), blood type, notes, a photo if you add one, and measurements. You provide this; the app asks for nothing it does not show you asking for. It is stored for the same reason as everything else — so your family's log is useful — and it is deleted when you delete the child's profile or the account, as described below.

Ridni is for adults keeping a record of their family. You must be 18 or older to create an account. We do not create accounts for children and the app is not addressed to them.

Photos

You can add a profile photo for yourself and for each child, taken with the camera or chosen from your photos. The app reads only the one photo you pick, and a photo taken with the camera is not saved to your gallery. Before it leaves the phone it is cropped to a small square (256 × 256 pixels, at most 100 KB); it is stored with your other records on our server in Germany, and only the people in your family see it. A child's photo can be set by the owner or a parent; an adult's photo only by that adult. Removing a photo deletes it, and it is also deleted with the child's profile or with your account.

Cycle data

If you turn on cycle tracking, the entries are stored like your other records, but only you can see them until you turn sharing on yourself. Cycle reminders are worked out on your phone and never leave it. This is health data, and it is treated as the most private thing in the app: the sharing switch is off until you move it, and turning it off again stops the sharing.

Health records

Temperatures, medications and vaccinations are records you keep, not assessments the app makes. Ridni has no medicine database and no vaccination schedule: it does not tell you whether a value is normal, whether a dose is safe, or whether you are behind on anything. The growth chart shows the WHO percentile band and the pregnancy weight chart shows the recommended range of weight gain (IOM) — reference lines beside your entries, not verdicts. If you make a PDF for a doctor, the app makes it on your phone and you decide where it goes; we do not receive a copy.

Email

We send you email only when the app needs to: a sign-up code, a password reset, a code when you change your address and a note to the old address once it has changed, and two letters about deleting your account — when it is scheduled and when it is done. These are sent through our email provider, Resend, from noreply@mail.ridni.app. We do not send newsletters or marketing.

Payments

If you buy a subscription, the payment is handled by Apple or Google. We never see your card or bank details; we receive only whether a subscription is active and when it renews, so the app can unlock what you paid for.

Notifications

If you turn reminders on, your phone gives us a push token — an identifier for that installation, which is how a reminder reaches that phone and not someone else's. Push messages are delivered through Apple Push Notification service on iPhone and Firebase Cloud Messaging on Android. The token is deleted with your account. Timer and cycle reminders are made by your phone itself and need no connection; the daily summary and "your timer was stopped" come from the server, so those two do. If you are in more than one family, a notification names the one it is about.

The Lock Screen and widgets

While a timer is running your phone can show it on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island, and a Home Screen widget can show the time since the last entries and start a sleep. For that the phone keeps its own copy of the children's names, the running timers and those times in a container the app and the widget share — on the phone, not on our server, and nothing new leaves it. What the Lock Screen shows is the least that identifies anyone: with one child, no name and no initial at all; with several, the avatar colour and the first letter. The child's name appears there only if you turn it on in Privacy, and a photo never does.

Crash reports

When the app closes unexpectedly, or runs into an error it could not handle, it sends Firebase Crashlytics a report of what went wrong: the device model, the operating system version, and where in the code it failed. The report includes an identifier for the installation, so that a repeated error can be recognised as one problem. It does not include your name, your email, or anything you have recorded about your children. Crash reports are what makes a bug fixable rather than guessable.

Server logs

To find faults, our server keeps a short record of requests that failed or were slow, and of sign-in and setup requests: the account identifier, which part of the service was called, the result and the time. It does not record your IP address or anything you entered. These records are deleted after 90 days.

What the app does not do

  • No advertising, and no advertising identifiers.
  • No analytics or usage tracking, beyond the crash reports described above.
  • No selling or sharing of your data with anyone, for any purpose.
  • No location: the app never asks for it and does not use it.
  • No other photos: the app opens the camera or your photos only when you add a profile photo, and reads nothing else.
  • No profiling and no automated decisions about you.

Where your data is

Your account and records are stored on servers run by DigitalOcean in Frankfurt, Germany. The services we use to run Ridni, and only for that:

  • DigitalOcean — hosting and database (data centre in Germany).
  • Resend — sending the emails described above (EU region, Ireland).
  • Google Firebase — push messages on Android and crash reports.
  • Apple — push messages on iPhone, and Sign in with Apple if you use it.
  • Google — Sign in with Google, if you use it.

Some of these companies are based in the United States, and Ridni is run from Ukraine, so some data can be accessed from outside the European Economic Area. Where that happens it is covered by the European Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses or, for US providers that have joined it, the EU–US Data Privacy Framework.

How long it is kept

Your records stay as long as your family uses them. Deleting a record takes it out of the app, with five seconds to undo. A deleted record is marked deleted rather than removed from the database at once, because every phone in the family has to learn that it is gone — otherwise a phone that was offline would sync it back. Thirty days after it was deleted, the record is removed for good.

Deleting your account schedules it: for seven days, signing in restores everything. After seven days a job runs and the account is anonymised — your name is replaced, your email address is replaced with one that goes nowhere, your password is erased, your push token and notification settings are deleted, and any Google or Apple connection is revoked. The account row itself stays, with nothing in it that identifies you, so that the family's records do not lose what they are attached to.

The records you added stay with your family — because they are the family's log of their own children, and removing them would take away another parent's history — and they show as added by a former member, with no name attached.

What is about you goes with your account: your cycle entries, your own weight and height, your appointments, your pumping sessions, the pregnancy entries you made, your profile photo and the list of your phones. They are about you rather than the children, so they are removed when the account is anonymised.

Database backups are kept for up to seven days, so anything removed can remain in a backup for at most seven more days before it is gone from there too.

Your rights

Under the GDPR you can ask us to give you a copy of your data, correct it, delete it, limit what we do with it, or object to it; you can withdraw consent where you gave it; and you can ask for your data in a portable form.

This version of the app has no export button, so a copy of your data is a request, not a download (the PDF for a doctor covers one child's health records, not everything): use the form at ridni.app/support or write to privacy@ridni.app. We answer within one month. We will ask you to write from the address on the account, because that is the only way we can tell it is yours.

You can also complain to a data protection authority: in Ukraine, the Ukrainian Parliament Commissioner for Human Rights; in the EU, the authority of the country you live in (list of EU authorities).
