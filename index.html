Everything about your child, in one place your family shares

Ridni is a private record of a child's day — feeds, sleep, nappies, growth, health — kept by the adults who are actually raising them. No feed, no ranking, no advice. Just what happened, and when.

App Store — soon Google Play — soon

Free to use. Seven languages. Works offline.

One record, every adult

A grandmother writes down a feed and it is on the other phones a second later. Nobody asks "did you already…?" at two in the morning.

Logging takes one tap

Timers for sleep and feeding, a quick record sheet for everything else. The app is designed to be used with one hand, in the dark, holding a baby.

Growth, with the WHO band

Weight and height drawn against the published 3rd–97th percentile range. The app shows the range and your child's line — it never tells you whether that is good.

Pregnancy and cycle, kept private

A pregnancy is shared with the family; a cycle is yours alone and never appears on anyone else's screen, not even as a number.

What it does not do

It does not score your child, compare them with other children, or tell you that a feed is overdue. It does not sell what you write down, and it carries no advertising and no trackers — the privacy policy says so in plain words, and is short enough to read.

When the phone goes across the desk

A doctor's view shows the records for one child and nothing else — no navigation, no other children, no way back into the app without your passcode. The data never leaves the phone to make it.
